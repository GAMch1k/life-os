# Calendar service

Calendar owns events and calendar sources in `calendar_db` and is their only source of truth: clients (the Dashboard, MCP, the Inbox processor) use its API, never its database. Gateway base: `/api/calendar`. The contract is `services/calendar/api/openapi.yaml`; `/health` and `/ready` are available.

Integrations (`google-calendar`, `calendar-feeds`) are separate services that sync their sources' events into Calendar and follow the events changed in Life OS through the [Calendar integration API](calendar-integration-api.md) (`/v1/integrations`, P11-06). ICS feeds, the ZAW waste calendar among them, are the [calendar-feeds](calendar-feeds.md) service's since P11-08, and Google Calendar is the [google-calendar](google-calendar.md) service's since P11-07: Calendar downloads no feed and calls no Google API.

## Events

An event has a `title`, optional `description` (notes/details) and `location`, its time (below), optional `recurrence` and `reminders`, a `priority`, a `status`, and a `source`. Calendar Events are not Tasks.

**Time.** A timed event (`allDay: false`) has `startAt`/`endAt`, RFC 3339 instants: any offset is accepted, and they are stored and returned in UTC (`2026-11-17T09:30:00+01:00` comes back as `2026-11-17T08:30:00Z`); `endAt` must be after `startAt`. It may carry `timeZone`, the IANA zone it was scheduled in (for example an ICS `TZID`), as a display hint. An all-day event (`allDay: true`) has `startDate`/`endDate`, local dates with `endDate` inclusive (a one-day event has both equal; ICS and FullCalendar use an exclusive end, so adapters subtract or add a day). All-day dates mean those days in the owner's timezone and take no `timeZone`.

**Owner's timezone.** Reads that compare all-day dates with instants (listing, search) take a `timeZone` parameter and default to `LIFE_OS_TIMEZONE` (set for the Calendar container in `compose.yaml`; unset or unknown means UTC, logged at startup). An all-day event spans midnight of its first day to midnight after its last day in that zone, so daylight-saving changes are respected. The Dashboard passes the timezone it shows the owner.

**Status.** `active` or `cancelled`. Cancelling (`PATCH {"status":"cancelled"}`) keeps the event as history: it stays readable by id but leaves listings and search unless `includeCancelled=true`; `cancelledAt` records when. `PATCH {"status":"active"}` restores it. `DELETE` removes an event permanently.

**Source.** `source.kind` is `manual` (created in Life OS) or `external` (brought in by an integration, see the [integration API](calendar-integration-api.md); `source.integration`, `source.provider`, and `source.readOnly` say from where and whether it accepts changes). The legacy kinds `ics` and `google` of Calendar's former in-process adapters are gone since P11-09 (`sourceKind=ics` is a 400). Imported events also carry `source.sourceId` (the Calendar source they came from, see [Sources](#sources)), `source.name` (that source's current name, such as "ZAW", which the Dashboard shows as the event's origin), and `source.externalUid` (their identity there, as the integration names it: for a feed the ICS `UID`, or `<UID>#<RECURRENCE-ID>` for one occurrence of a recurring ICS event); a source holds each `externalUid` once, so syncs update in place. The API only creates `manual` events (a `source` in a request body is ignored) and updates never change the source. **Feed events are read-only:** a `PATCH` that names anything but their reminders (`reminders`, `useDefaultReminders`) and `priority`, and any `DELETE`, return 409 `managed_by_source`; only their source changes, cancels (`removedFromSourceAt`), and restores them (a feed's through calendar-feeds' syncs). An integration's event whose source accepts changes (such as a Google calendar the account may edit) is changed through the API like a Life OS event, unless it is read-only on its own (`readOnlyReason`, such as one occurrence of a Google rule Calendar cannot hold); the integration writes the change to its provider through the change feed, asynchronously, and the provider's later version wins a conflict. `editable` and `readOnlyReason` say what an event allows. The legacy `externalReference` (`provider`/`externalId`) is still accepted and returned but deprecated.

**Reminders** reach the owner: Calendar sends each effective reminder through Notifications when it is due (see [Reminder delivery](#reminder-delivery)). Each reminder is either `{"kind":"relative","minutesBefore":30}` (before the start; an all-day event starts at owner-local midnight) or `{"kind":"localTime","daysBefore":1,"localTime":"19:00"}` (at 19:00 on the owner's wall clock, one calendar day before the event's start date, resolved in the owner's timezone when due, so a pickup on 17 November at 06:00 reminds on 16 November at 19:00, whatever daylight saving does). They work like Google Calendar's notifications: an event follows its calendar's default reminders while `useDefaultReminders` is true (the default) and adds any number of its own (`reminders`, at most 10) on top, so "1 day before, 3 hours before, 30 minutes before" can all apply at once. The defaults are an imported event's source's `defaultReminders` and, for an event created in Life OS, the owner's defaults from [settings](#settings), one list for timed and one for all-day events. Every returned event has `reminders` (its own), `useDefaultReminders`, `defaultReminders` (the defaults for this event, whether it uses them or not), and `effectiveReminders`: the defaults when used, then its own, each reminder once. Every effective reminder is sent.

**Priority** says how important an event is, `low`, `medium`, or `high`, and so how important its reminder notifications are. Every returned event has `priority`, the one that applies, and `defaultPriority`, the one it has without a priority of its own: its source's `defaultPriority` for an imported event (medium unless the source sets another), `medium` for an event created in Life OS. The effective rule is: the event's own priority, else `defaultPriority` (for an occurrence of a recurring event: its own, else the series'). Create, `PATCH`, This event, and This and following take `priority`; `null` is refused (400 `priority cannot be null`), and setting the default is how an event goes back to it. Calendar stores an event's own priority (`events.priority`) only while it differs from its default, so an imported event follows later changes of its source's default unless the owner gave it another priority, which refreshes keep, like the owner's reminders. Sources store `default_priority` (migration 000011; existing events have no own priority and read as medium, existing sources default to medium). A priority change is an ordinary update in Activity (`changes: ["priority"]`).

### Settings

`GET /v1/settings` returns the owner's default reminders for the events they create, `defaultReminders.timed` and `defaultReminders.allDay` (at most 10 each, none until first set), and `updatedAt` (null until the first change). `PATCH /v1/settings` replaces the lists it names, for example `{"defaultReminders":{"timed":[{"kind":"relative","minutesBefore":30}]}}`; every Life OS event that uses its defaults follows. The settings live in `calendar_settings`, a single row. Changing them, like changing a source's configuration, is not recorded in Activity.

**Recurrence** (`frequency` `daily`/`weekly`/`monthly`/`yearly`, `interval`, and optionally `byWeekday`, `count`, or `until`) is stored once with the event (the series) and follows iCalendar (RFC 5545) rules: a timed event keeps its wall-clock time in its `timeZone` (else the owner's) across daylight-saving changes. Weekly repeats on the start's weekday, or on each of `byWeekday` (`MO`…`SU`, weekly only, must include the start's weekday in the event's timezone; returned in week order). Monthly repeats on the start's day of the month and skips months without it (the 31st); yearly repeats on the start's date, so an all-day event on 17 September repeats "annually on September 17" (a birthday; 29 February only in leap years). A series ends after `count` occurrences (1–1000, "ends after N times"), or at `until` (the latest start of an occurrence, inclusive, not before the event's start; an all-day series compares its dates with until's date in the owner's timezone), or never; not `count` and `until` together. Listing and search **expand** recurring events (below). Life OS events recur this way, and so do an integration's series (such as a Google recurring event whose rule fits the model).

### Recurring events: scopes and occurrence changes

A change to a recurring event has one of three scopes, as in Google Calendar:

- **This event**: `PATCH /v1/events/{id}/occurrences/{recurrenceId}` changes one occurrence: title, description, location (`null` clears them for this occurrence), its start and end (the series' kind: `startAt`/`endAt` or `startDate`/`endDate`), reminders and `useDefaultReminders`, `priority`, or `status`: `cancelled` skips it (kept as history), `active` brings it back. The occurrence's own changes are stored as an **exception** (`event_exceptions`: only the fields it overrides, explicit nulls for cleared notes or location, and `cancelled_at` while skipped), keyed by its `recurrenceId`, which stays its original start even when it is moved. A field set back to the series' value stops being an override, and an occurrence with nothing of its own left has no exception. `allDay`, `recurrence`, and `timeZone` belong to the series (400). The write checks that `recurrenceId` is a real occurrence (404 `occurrence not found`; a non-recurring event is 404 `this event does not repeat`).
- **This and following**: `POST .../{recurrenceId}/split` ends the series with the occurrence before (its `until` becomes that occurrence's start, or for an all-day series midnight of its day in the owner's timezone, and `count` is removed) and creates a new series (new id) starting at the occurrence's original time with the body's changes (an `UpdateEventRequest`; times in the body are the new start; without `recurrence` it repeats like the old one, a `count` keeping the occurrences still to come; `recurrence: null` makes it a single event). On the first occurrence nothing is split: the body changes the series itself (200, like `PATCH /v1/events/{id}`). `POST .../{recurrenceId}/truncate` deletes this and following: the series ends before the occurrence and the exceptions from it on are removed; on the first occurrence the series is deleted.
- **All events**: `PATCH /v1/events/{id}` and `DELETE /v1/events/{id}` on the series (`recurrence: null` stops it repeating: it keeps its first occurrence's times and loses every exception).

`recurrenceId` in these paths is accepted URL-encoded or with literal colons (the Dashboard's proxy decodes path segments), and a timed one in any RFC 3339 offset.

Exceptions survive changes as long as their occurrence exists, meaning the series still has an occurrence with the same original start. An all-events change of the start, the kind, the time zone, or the rule removes the exceptions of occurrences it ends or moves (a new start time moves them all; a later `until` or extra weekdays keep them); a title or length change keeps every exception, and an exception's own override wins over the series' new value. A split moves the exceptions from the occurrence on to the new series when their occurrence still exists there (the same time, kind, time zone, and rule) and drops the others; the fields the split body sets replace the split occurrence's own changes to them. Deleting a series deletes its exceptions. These writes run in one transaction holding the event row, so a concurrent series change cannot leave an exception of an occurrence that no longer exists.

Recurring feed events are stored one event per occurrence and are not series: the occurrence endpoints answer 409 `managed_by_source` for writes and 404 for reads. An integration's series whose source accepts changes (a Google recurring event whose rule fits the model) takes the same scopes; a split puts its new series in the same source under `lifeos:<id>` until the integration re-keys it. An occurrence expanded from a rule Calendar cannot hold is read-only.

## Listing, search, and occurrences

`GET /v1/events?from&to` returns the events overlapping `[from, to)`, soonest start first. A recurring event appears once per occurrence that overlaps the range, in its place in the order: the item is the recurring event (same `id`, title, recurrence, and so on) with that occurrence's `startAt`/`endAt` (or `startDate`/`endDate`) and `occurrence: {seriesId, recurrenceId, modified}`, where `recurrenceId` is the occurrence's original start (a UTC instant, or a date for all-day events) and is unique within the series. Clients that key items should use `id` plus `occurrence.recurrenceId`. Each occurrence's own changes apply: a moved occurrence is listed at its new time (also when it moved into the range from outside it, and not when it moved out), an edited one with its own fields and effective reminders, both with `modified: true`; a skipped occurrence is left out unless `includeCancelled`, then listed with `status: cancelled` and `cancelledAt`. Ordering and cursors use an occurrence's time after its own changes. Non-recurring events are returned as stored, without `occurrence`. Paging (`limit`, `nextCursor`) runs over occurrences and events alike, so Home, MCP, and the Calendar UI see the same items. `GET /v1/events/{id}/occurrences/{recurrenceId}` returns one occurrence as listed, whatever its status. MCP's Calendar tools answer events with their times on the owner's clock, and `calendar_update`'s `move` ("an hour later", "to tomorrow, same time") reads an event, or with a `recurrenceId` one occurrence, and patches its new start and end through these endpoints, keeping its length; see [Task and event details and moves](mcp.md#task-and-event-details-and-moves).

`GET /v1/events/search?q` matches every word of `q` in the title, location, or description. A matching recurring event is returned as occurrences too: every occurrence in the range when both `from` and `to` are given, otherwise one item, its next occurrence that is not over yet (or its last one, if it has ended). Occurrences carry their own changes as in listing, and a skipped occurrence is passed over (the next one stands for the series) unless `includeCancelled`. Words are matched against the series' title, location, and description only, not against one occurrence's own changes to them.

Recurring ICS events are not stored as rules: calendar-feeds imports each occurrence as its own event (see [calendar-feeds](calendar-feeds.md#refresh)), so they list like any other event.

## Sources

A **source** is a calendar whose events Calendar holds for someone else: an integration's (`kind` `external`, see the [integration API](calendar-integration-api.md)), such as each ICS feed of [calendar-feeds](calendar-feeds.md) (ZAW among them), or each Google calendar the owner syncs in [google-calendar](google-calendar.md). Every source shares the reconciliation and the listing filters (`sourceKind`, `sourceId`).

`/v1/sources` serves the **owner's settings** of the integrations' sources: `name` (the label shown with its events, such as "ZAW"), `enabled` (a disabled source refuses its integration's syncs and keeps its events), `defaultReminders`, and `defaultPriority` (the priority of its events that have none of their own; `medium` unless set). `integration`, `externalId`, `provider`, `readOnly`, and `privateLocation` (its events' locations stay out of reminders, such as the ZAW feed's, which are the owner's street) say whose it is and are set by the integration. A feed's URL, type filter, preset, and refresh state belong to calendar-feeds (`/api/integrations/calendar-feeds/v1/feeds`): they are not part of a Calendar source, and feeds are added, refreshed, and unsubscribed there. Deleting a source here deletes its events (and calendar-feeds then drops the feed); set `enabled: false` instead to stop syncing and keep them.

**History: the handovers and the contract (P11-06 to P11-09).** Until P11-08 Calendar refreshed the feeds itself (kind `ics`, with the ZAW preset), and until P11-07 it synced Google itself (kind `google`, `/v1/google/*`, the `google_*` tables). P11-06 (migration 000014) gave every source its integration identity and added the integration API beside the in-process adapters (`in_process`); migrations 000015 (`calendar_feeds_handover`) and 000016 (`google_calendar_handover`) turned those sources and their events into kind `external`, out of process, and the integrations copied the legacy data into their own databases once. Migration 000017 (`calendar_contract`, P11-09) then removed what only the adapters used, see [Contract (P11-09)](#contract-p11-09).

### Contract (P11-09)

Migration 000017 first checks that Calendar holds nothing an integration has not taken over: it raises an exception, changing nothing, while any source is in process, of kind `ics` or `google`, or without its integration identity (`integration`, `externalId`, `provider`), or any event is of kind `ics` or `google` (golang-migrate then leaves the version dirty at 17; fix the data, `migrate force 16`, and migrate again). Otherwise it:

- drops the tables `google_connections`, `google_calendars`, `google_oauth_states`, `google_events`, and `google_exports`;
- drops the `calendar_sources` columns `url`, `filter_include`, `filter_exclude`, the refresh state (`next_refresh_at`, `refreshing_until`, `last_attempt_at`, `last_success_at`, `last_outcome`, `last_error`, `last_counts`, `consecutive_failures`), `etag`, `last_modified`, `preset`, `preset_config`, `google_calendar_id`, and `in_process`, with their checks and indexes;
- adds `private_location`, true for the ZAW feed's source (the legacy preset, or a calendar-feeds source labelled ZAW), which reminders read instead of the preset;
- makes `integration`, `external_id`, and `provider` NOT NULL and narrows the kinds: sources `external`, events `manual` or `external`;
- replaces `calendar_event_followed` so the change feed follows the events of sources that accept changes, without `in_process`.

It keeps `integration_config` (what the integration last sent of the owner's settings, so a registration changes only what the integration changed since), and every source, event, exception, reminder, and priority as they are; it writes nothing to the change feed. Its down migration rebuilds the old structure (empty tables, empty legacy columns, the old checks, sources out of process) without the data; take a `pg_dump` of `calendar_db` before migrating.

## Google Calendar

Google Calendar is the [google-calendar](google-calendar.md) integration service since P11-07: the OAuth connection, the calendar list, the inbound sync, writing the owner's edits to Google, and the "Life OS" calendar all moved there, with its own database; it reaches Calendar only through the [integration API](calendar-integration-api.md) and the events API, as `system:google-calendar`. Each Google calendar it syncs is a source here (`kind` `external`, integration `google-calendar`, `externalId` the Google calendar id, provider `Google`, read-only unless the account may edit the calendar), so its events are external events with `source.provider` `Google` and `source.name` the calendar's name. Calendar has no Google code, table, or setting (see the history under [Sources](#sources)).

## Reminder delivery

Calendar sends every effective reminder of every active event and occurrence to the owner through [Notifications](notifications.md) (`pkg/notify`, the one notification path) when it is due. The owner can add as many reminders as they like ("1 day before, 3 hours before, 30 minutes before") and each one is sent. There are no quiet hours.

- **Dispatcher.** A goroutine in the Calendar process (`internal/reminders`, beside the change feed pruning) runs every `CALENDAR_REMINDER_INTERVAL_SECONDS` (30 s). Each run computes the reminders due in `(last run, now]`. It reads the events and occurrences that start from a day before that window to 29 days after it (the largest reminder offset is 28 days), with indexed queries for single events and the same recurrence expansion and occurrence exceptions as listing (`ReminderOccurrences` in `internal/api`), and their `effectiveReminders` resolved as listing resolves them: an imported event's source defaults (ZAW: the evening before at 19:00; a Google calendar: its default reminders), a Life OS event's owner defaults for timed or all-day events, plus its own, each once.
- **Due times.** `relative`: the start minus `minutesBefore`, an exact duration; an all-day event starts at owner-local midnight of its first day. `localTime`: `localTime` on the owner's wall clock on the start's owner-local date minus `daysBefore`, resolved in `LIFE_OS_TIMEZONE`, so the evening-before reminder stays at 19:00 across daylight saving changes. A wall time skipped when the clocks go forward is the same time after the jump (02:30 becomes 03:30); one that occurs twice is the first.
- **Occurrences and changes.** Every occurrence of a recurring event reminds on its own. A skipped occurrence sends nothing; one with its own changes uses its own times, title, and reminders. Cancelled and deleted events send nothing. Changes take effect at the next run: a moved event or occurrence reminds again at its new time (a reminder already sent for the old time stays sent), and a reminder added before its time fires. A reminder whose time has already passed when it is added, or when its event is moved, is not sent.
- **Exactly once.** Each reminder's dedupe key is `calendar:reminder:<eventId>:<recurrenceId or single>:<start>:<reminder>`, where start is the occurrence's start instant in UTC or its first date, and reminder is `rel:30` or `local:1:19:00`. Notifications stores a key once. Calendar also keeps its cursor (`calendar_reminder_dispatch.last_run_at`) and every handled key (`calendar_reminder_log`: sent, missed, or failed; pruned after 30 days), so restarts and overlapping runs neither repeat nor skip a reminder. One process runs at a time: a run holds a lease on the state row (two minutes, at least four intervals), and another Calendar process skips its run meanwhile.
- **Failures.** When Notifications is unreachable or answers 5xx, the run stops at that reminder and keeps the cursor just before it, so the next run retries it; reminders already sent are not sent again. A notification Notifications refuses (4xx) is recorded as failed, logged, and not retried. Either error is kept as `lastError`.
- **Missed while down.** After Calendar was down, the first run handles everything due since the last run, at most 7 days back. A reminder up to `CALENDAR_REMINDER_GRACE_MINUTES` (120) late is sent, phrased from the time it is sent and ending with "(late)"; an older one is not notified but recorded in Activity as `calendar.reminder.missed`. A reminder counts as late when it is sent more than two intervals (at least a minute) after its time. The very first run after deployment only starts the cursor.
- **Off without Notifications.** With `NOTIFICATIONS_SERVICE_URL` unset the dispatcher does not run (logged at startup). Once it is set, reminders that came due meanwhile are handled like those due while Calendar was down.

| Notification field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `title` | The event's (or occurrence's) title. |
| `body` | When the event starts, seen from the reminder time, in the owner's timezone with a 24-hour clock, then its location: "In 30 minutes · 15:00–16:00 · Dr. Weber", "In 3 hours · 15:00–16:00", "Tomorrow at 15:00 · Dr. Weber", "Thu 15 Oct at 09:00"; all-day "Tomorrow, all day", "Today until Sun 18 Oct". Events of a source whose locations are private (`privateLocation`, such as the ZAW feed, whose location is the owner's street) leave it out: "Papier" with "Tomorrow at 06:00". |
| `importance` | The event's effective `priority`, `low`, `medium`, or `high` (its own, for an occurrence its own or else the series', else its `defaultPriority`: its source's for an imported event, `medium` for a Life OS one). |
| `category` | `calendar.reminder` |
| `link` | `/calendar?date=<the start's owner-local date>` |
| `occurredAt` | The reminder time. |
| `dedupeKey` | As above. |

`calendar.reminder.missed` has subject `calendar_event` (the event's id), actor `system:calendar`, outcome `failed`, `occurredAt` the reminder time, a summary such as "Reminder for “Standup” due Sat 10 Oct 2026, 12:10 CEST was missed: Calendar was not running", and `data` with the `title`, `dueAt`, the start, the reminder, `recurrenceId`, `sourceKind`/`sourceId`, and `dedupeKey`; its id is derived from the dedupe key, so it is recorded once. A sent reminder records nothing in Calendar's name: Notifications records `notification.created`.

`GET /v1/reminders/status` reports the delivery's health for the Status page: `enabled`, `intervalSeconds`, `graceMinutes`, `handledThrough` (the cursor), `lastRunAt`, `lastSuccessAt`, `lastError` with `lastErrorAt` (current while later than `lastSuccessAt`), and `lastSentAt`. The Status page shows it as the Event reminders part of the Calendar check (see [Dashboard](../frontend/dashboard.md)).

`internal/reminders` holds unit tests (due times of relative and localTime reminders across both daylight saving changes, skipped and repeated wall times, all-day events, the window, dedupe keys, bodies and late phrasing, importance) and integration tests against `CALENDAR_TEST_DATABASE_URL` with an in-process Notifications that deduplicates like the real one: every effective reminder sent once, recurring occurrences with skipped and moved exceptions, all-day and imported (ZAW and another feed) defaults, an integration's events (a Google calendar's defaults plus the event's own, a series with an occurrence cancelled in Google), importance from the effective priority (own, a source's default, an occurrence's own), late and missed reminders after downtime, a lost cursor, failing and refusing Notifications, moves, cancellations, deletions, added reminders, the lease and two processes at once, and migration 000012 down and up.

## Activity

When `ACTIVITY_SERVICE_URL` is set, every event change made through the API records exactly one Activity event once it is stored, with subject `calendar_event` and the event's id. It is attributed to the request's `X-Life-OS-Actor` (`user` for the owner in the Dashboard, `agent:<name>` for the AI through MCP, `dev:<name>` for development tools) and carries the request's `X-Correlation-ID` and `X-Causation-ID`, so a change the agent makes appears in its capture's workflow in the Event Log, after the MCP action that caused it.

| Type | Recorded for |
| --- | --- |
| `calendar.event.created` | `POST /v1/events`. Its id is derived from the event's, so an idempotent replay is recorded once. |
| `calendar.event.rescheduled` | A `PATCH` that changes the start, the end, or all-day (with whatever else it changes, unless it also changes the status). |
| `calendar.event.cancelled` / `calendar.event.restored` | A `PATCH` that changes `status`. |
| `calendar.event.updated` | Any other `PATCH` that changes something: title, notes, location, time zone, repetition, reminders, or priority (also an imported event's reminders and priority). |
| `calendar.event.deleted` | `DELETE /v1/events/{id}`, or a truncate at a series' first occurrence. |

A change of one occurrence (This event) records `calendar.event.cancelled`, `restored`, `rescheduled`, or `updated` on the series with `data.recurrenceId` and that occurrence's times, such as "Event “Gym” on Sat 10 Oct 2026, 07:00 CEST cancelled". A split records `calendar.event.updated` on the old series (`changes: ["recurrence"]`) and `calendar.event.created` for the new one with `data.splitFrom` (the old series' id); a truncate records `calendar.event.updated` (`changes: ["recurrence"]`).

Changes the google-calendar integration makes through the events API (a change made in Google to an exported Life OS event, applied here) record `calendar.event.updated`, `rescheduled`, or `cancelled` attributed to `system:google-calendar`; the integration records its own events (`calendar.google.*`, see [google-calendar](google-calendar.md#activity)).

A `PATCH` that changes nothing, and any refused request (invalid, 404, 409 `managed_by_source`), records nothing as an event change. `data` holds the `title`, the times (`startAt`/`endAt` or `startDate`/`endDate`), `allDay`, `timeZone`, `status`, `sourceKind` and `sourceId`, and `recurrence`; an update adds `changes` (the changed fields) and, when renamed, `previousTitle`; a reschedule adds `previous` (the old times). Notes, location, and reminders are not copied into Activity. The summary is a sentence with times in the owner's timezone, such as "Event “Dentist” moved to Thu 19 Nov 2026, 09:00 CET".

An integration's sync records nothing here: the integration records its own outcome (calendar-feeds: one `calendar.source.refreshed` or `refresh_failed` per feed refresh, see [calendar-feeds](calendar-feeds.md#activity)). google-calendar: one only when a Google calendar's sync changed something or failed, and `calendar.source.removed` when its events leave Life OS (see [google-calendar](google-calendar.md#activity)).

Reminder delivery records only reminders it could not send in time, as `calendar.reminder.missed` (see [Reminder delivery](#reminder-delivery)).

## API

| Request | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| `POST /v1/events` | Creates an active manual event (201). Requires `title` and `allDay` plus that kind's start and end; optional `priority` (default medium). `Idempotency-Key`: an identical repeat returns the original event; reuse with a different body is 409. |
| `GET /v1/events/{id}` | The event, whatever its status. |
| `PATCH /v1/events/{id}` | All events for a series (see [scopes](#recurring-events-scopes-and-occurrence-changes)). Changes only the named fields; `null` clears a nullable field (`description`, `location`, `timeZone`, `recurrence`, `reminders`, and the start/end fields). Changing `allDay` switches kind: the other kind's fields are cleared and the new kind's start and end must be given. The merged event is validated as a whole, holding the event so concurrent changes wait for each other. `reminders: null` returns the event to its defaults alone (no own reminders, `useDefaultReminders` true unless the body sets it). Read-only imported events (feeds, Google calendars the account can only read, occurrences of rules Calendar cannot hold): only `reminders`, `useDefaultReminders`, and `priority` (else 409 `managed_by_source`). An integration follows the change through the change feed (a Life OS event's export, an editable Google event's write to Google). |
| `DELETE /v1/events/{id}` | Deletes a Life OS event, or an integration's event whose source accepts changes, permanently (204), for a series every occurrence and exception (All events); integrations follow the deletion through the change feed (a copy in Google is deleted, a Google event deleted there). Read-only imported events return 409 `managed_by_source`. |
| `GET /v1/events/{id}/occurrences/{recurrenceId}` | One occurrence with its own changes applied, whatever its status; 404 `this event does not repeat` or `occurrence not found`. |
| `PATCH /v1/events/{id}/occurrences/{recurrenceId}` | This event: the occurrence's own changes or skip (`status`); returns the occurrence. 400 for `allDay` other than the series', `recurrence`, or `timeZone`; 409 `managed_by_source` for read-only imported events; an integration writes an editable series' occurrence change to its provider through the change feed. |
| `POST /v1/events/{id}/occurrences/{recurrenceId}/split` | This and following: ends the series before the occurrence and returns the new series (201), or on the first occurrence changes and returns the series (200). |
| `POST /v1/events/{id}/occurrences/{recurrenceId}/truncate` | Deletes this and following (204); on the first occurrence deletes the series. |
| `GET /v1/events?from&to` | Events and occurrences overlapping `[from, to)` (`to` must be after `from`), soonest start first. Optional `timeZone`, `includeCancelled`, `sourceKind`, `sourceId`, `editable`, `limit` (1–100, default 50), and `cursor`. |
| `GET /v1/events/search?q` | Events whose title, description, or location contain every word of `q` (case-insensitive substring, up to 10 words), across all dates. Optional `from`/`to` (either may be open), `timeZone`, `includeCancelled`, `sourceKind`, `sourceId`, `editable`, `limit` (1–100, default 20), and `cursor`. |
| `GET /v1/sources` | Every source (`kind` `external`, such as the feeds of calendar-feeds and the Google calendars of google-calendar) with the owner's settings and its identity in its integration; the rest of its configuration and its sync state stay with the integration. |
| `/v1/integrations/{integration}/sources/{externalId}` (`GET`, `PUT`, `DELETE`, `POST .../sync`) and `GET /v1/integrations/changes` | The [integration API](calendar-integration-api.md): register an integration's sources, sync their events, and follow the change feed (actor `system:<integration>` or `dev:<name>`). |
| `GET /v1/sources/{id}` | The source. |
| `PATCH /v1/sources/{id}` | Changes the owner's settings it names: `name`, `enabled`, `defaultReminders` (`null` removes them), `defaultPriority` (cannot be null). Other properties (such as a feed's `url` or `filter`, which belong to calendar-feeds) are not settings of a source: a body naming none of the four is a 400. The 409 `managed_by_integration` of the handover releases is gone. |
| `DELETE /v1/sources/{id}` | Deletes the source and its events (204). |
| `GET /v1/settings`, `PATCH /v1/settings` | The owner's default reminders for timed and all-day Life OS events (see [Settings](#settings)). |
| `GET /v1/reminders/status` | Reminder delivery's health (see [Reminder delivery](#reminder-delivery)). |

Search ranks a word found in the title 3, in the location 2, in the description 1, most relevant first; among equals, events not over yet come first (soonest first), then past ones (most recent first). Listing and search page with an opaque `nextCursor`: pass it back as `cursor` with the same parameters; no `nextCursor` means the last page. A search cursor also keeps the moment that split upcoming from past, so paging stays consistent while time passes.

Every write needs `X-Life-OS-Actor` (see [API overview](../api/overview.md)). Invalid requests return 400 with an error message saying what to fix; unknown ids return 404.

## Configuration

| Variable | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `LIFE_OS_TIMEZONE` | `UTC` | The owner's timezone (all-day matching, recurrence wall clock, the window of an integration's `upcomingOnly` sync). |
| `ACTIVITY_SERVICE_URL` | empty | Activity service for event changes; empty records nothing. |
| `NOTIFICATIONS_SERVICE_URL` | empty | The Notifications service reminders are sent to (compose: `http://notifications:8080`); empty turns [reminder delivery](#reminder-delivery) off. |
| `CALENDAR_REMINDER_INTERVAL_SECONDS` | `30` | How often due reminders are looked for (5 to 300). |
| `CALENDAR_REMINDER_GRACE_MINUTES` | `120` | How late a reminder is still sent after Calendar was down (10 to 1440); older ones are recorded as missed. |
| `CALENDAR_CHANGE_RETENTION_DAYS` | `30` | How long the [change feed](calendar-integration-api.md#change-feed) keeps its entries (1 to 3650); an older cursor gets 410 `cursor_expired`. |

The feed settings (`CALENDAR_SOURCE_REFRESH_AT`, `CALENDAR_ICS_*`, `CALENDAR_ZAW_ICS_URL`) moved to [calendar-feeds](calendar-feeds.md#configuration) with P11-08. The Google settings (`LIFE_OS_GOOGLE_*`, the sync interval) moved to [google-calendar](google-calendar.md#configuration) with P11-07.

## Tests

`internal/api`, `internal/repository`, `internal/recurrence`, and `internal/sources` hold unit tests (reconciliation, recurrence across daylight saving, weekday and count rules, occurrence exceptions, and how a split shares a series' occurrences out, also for count rules and across daylight saving; series, exception, and source-reminder reconciliation in `internal/sources`) and integration tests (among them the three change scopes over HTTP and migrations 000008 and 000010 up and down keeping events; `/v1/sources` serving an integration source's owner settings and no legacy field in `internal/api/sources_integration_test.go`). The integration tests run against the Postgres named by `CALENDAR_TEST_DATABASE_URL` (a throwaway database: each test creates and drops its own schema and applies every migration) and skip when it is unset. For example: Priority tests (own, default, a source's default and an owner's priority kept through an integration's syncs, an occurrence's own, Activity) are in `internal/api/priority_integration_test.go`, and migration 000011 up and down in `internal/repository/priority_integration_test.go`. The legacy Google migrations (000009, 000010, 000013) keep their tests in `internal/repository/google_integration_test.go`, run on the chain up to 000016. The integration API is tested in `internal/api/integration_integration_test.go` (registration and the owner's settings, `privateLocation`, authorization, snapshot and delta idempotency, removal and windows, series with exceptions, owner overrides kept, validation) and `internal/api/changes_integration_test.go` (the change feed: every change in commit order with its actor, a restart, pruning and an expired cursor, writable integration events, re-keying, tombstones, and scope); migration 000014 on live-shaped data, up and down, in `internal/repository/integration_migration_test.go`; the calendar feeds handover (000015) in `internal/repository/feeds_handover_migration_test.go`; the Google handover (000016: kinds, events, uids, skips, the owner's reminders and priority kept, the journal attributed to the integration, idempotency, down and up) in `internal/repository/handover_integration_test.go`; the contract (000017: on live-shaped data, what it drops and keeps, the ZAW source's private locations, the change feed after it, the narrowed kinds, down and up; its refusal, changing nothing, for each kind of data not handed over; and the whole chain down and up through it) in `internal/repository/contract_migration_test.go`. The Google mapping and two-way sync are tested in [google-calendar](google-calendar.md#code-and-tests), against the real Calendar.
